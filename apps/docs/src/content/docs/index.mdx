Most Android apps assume you can see them. EchoGuide removes that assumption. You say what
you want in Amharic or English, and it operates the real app on your behalf — tapping,
scrolling, and typing through Android's accessibility layer.

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Amharic first" icon="translate">
		Speech models from Addis AI, purpose-built for Amharic rather than
		retrofitted from English. Around **3% word error rate** on Amharic speech.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Drives real apps" icon="rocket">
		A native Kotlin `AccessibilityService` performs the taps and swipes, so
		EchoGuide works with third-party apps you already have installed.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Never silent" icon="approve-check">
		Every state is announced aloud. If you cannot see the screen, silence is
		indistinguishable from a crash — so EchoGuide treats it as one.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Stores nothing by default" icon="seti:lock">
		No audio and no transcripts are retained unless you explicitly opt in.
		[Read what that means](/reference/privacy/).
	</Card>
</CardGrid>
